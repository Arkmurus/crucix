# ARIA — Verified Combat Performance Database
Source: Arkmurus Hardware Library v1.0 | Classification: ARKMURUS INTERNAL

## ARIA Combat Verification Methodology

ARIA only cites combat performance that is:
1. Verified by at least 2 independent sources
2. Supported by physical evidence (Oryx, ACLED, OSMP)
3. Not solely from manufacturer or government claims

Primary verification source: Oryx open-source equipment loss database

## Ukraine Conflict (2022-Present) — Most Data-Rich Modern Conflict

### Key Findings (verified open-source data to 2025)
- T-72 variants: 3,000+ destroyed/captured (highest single-type loss)
- T-80: 400+ destroyed | T-90: 120+ destroyed
- Bradley IFV: <20 destroyed (far better survivability than BMP)
- BMP-2: 500+ destroyed (lower protection than Bradley)
- Leopard 2: 30+ destroyed in Ukrainian service (mostly A4 variants)
- M1 Abrams: 6 destroyed (deployed in limited numbers, 2024)
- HIMARS: 0 destroyed (Russian claimed destroyed; none verified)
- Bayraktar TB2: 20+ destroyed (effective early, degraded by EW/AD)

### Tactical Lessons for Procurement Advisory
1. ERA (Explosive Reactive Armour) significantly extends tank life
2. Active Protection Systems (Trophy, Arena) demonstrate effectiveness
3. Electronic warfare capability now essential alongside kinetic
4. Drone integration at every level is no longer optional
5. Logistics (ammunition supply) is as decisive as weapons themselves
6. Night vision and thermal sight advantage was decisive for Ukraine

## Nagorno-Karabakh (2020) — Drone Warfare Demonstration

Azerbaijan combined Bayraktar TB2 + Harop loitering munitions with conventional forces to defeat Armenian armour (T-72 tanks, BM-21, air defence systems) with minimal Azerbaijani aircraft losses.

Verified: 185+ Armenian tanks destroyed, 90+ APCs, 180+ artillery pieces

Lesson: Unmodernised Soviet-era armour without air defence is catastrophically vulnerable to precision drone attack.

CPLP relevance: CPLP African forces operate this exact profile of vulnerability — lesson directly applicable to capability gap identification for BD purposes.

## Mali / Sahel COIN (2012-Present) — African Operational Context

### French Operation Serval/Barkhane (2013-2022)
- Demonstrated: Light forces + air power (Rafale + Tiger helicopter) effective against non-state armed groups in Sahelian terrain
- Equipment that performed: Caracal SAR helicopter, Rafale, Caesar SPH
- Lesson: Strategic mobility (wheeled over tracked) critical in Sahel

Wagner Group (2022-): Demonstrated Russian COIN model replacing French — significant geopolitical shift relevant for CPLP markets

## Angolan Civil War (1975-2002) — CPLP Direct Relevance

### FAPLA (Government) vs UNITA/SADF
- Soviet equipment (T-54/55, BMP-1, BTR-60, MiG-21/23, Mi-24): Cuban and Soviet-supplied forces vs SADF and UNITA
- SADF equipment: Ratel, Casspir, G6 Rhino (combat debut in Angola) — proved effectiveness of South African COIN-oriented design
- Key battles: Cuito Cuanavale (1987-88) — largest land battle in Africa since WWII

Lesson for ARIA: Understanding Angolan equipment history is essential for maintenance, upgrade, and replacement advisory in FAPLA/FAA. The FAA (current Angolan armed forces) is the direct successor to FAPLA and retains much of the same equipment.

## Mozambique Cabo Delgado (2017-Present)

- Insurgency: Al-Shabaab (Mozambique) affiliated with ISIS
- Equipment used: Small arms (AK variants), RPG-7, IEDs, technicals
- SADC Mission (SAMIM 2021-): Light forces from regional states
- Rwandan contingent: Most effective foreign contribution (trained, motivated, light infantry + UAV ISR)

### Priority capability needs for Mozambique
1. Tactical ISR (small UAS)
2. Armoured mobility (MRAP for mine threat)
3. Maritime patrol (coast and river)
4. SOF capability (targeted operations)

ARIA procurement advisory: Mozambique is a live market for exactly these capability categories. Any client approaching MOZ defence should focus on MRAP, small UAS, and OPV categories.

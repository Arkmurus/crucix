# ARIA Military Hardware — Ammunition Intelligence
Source: Arkmurus Hardware Library v1.0 | Classification: ARKMURUS INTERNAL

## ARIA Ammunition Doctrine

Ammunition is the most critical logistical factor in any procurement. ARIA always assesses:
1. Calibre compatibility with existing inventory
2. Production sources and supply chain security
3. Export control classification (EAR / ITAR / Wassenaar ML3)
4. Stockpile life and storage requirements
5. Cost per round at scale
6. Availability from multiple suppliers (single-source risk)

## Small Calibre Ammunition

### 5.56x45mm NATO (SS109 / M855)
- Velocity: 930 m/s | Energy: 1,797J | NATO standard since 1980
- Penetration: 3mm steel at 600m | Fragmentation: Yes (at 800 fps impact)
- Producers: Vista Outdoor (USA), BAE Radway Green (UK), Nammo (Norway), CBC (Brazil), Denel (South Africa), IMI (Israel)
- Export: Generally EAR99 or ECCN 0A984; NATO country transfers easier
- CPLP relevance: Required for any NATO-pattern weapon supply to African forces; CBC Brazil is a key supplier for Portuguese-speaking markets

### 5.45x39mm (Soviet/Russian standard)
- Velocity: 880 m/s | Energy: 1,316J | Warsaw Pact standard since 1974
- Producers: Russia (Tula Arsenal, Barnaul), Ukraine, Bulgaria, Romania
- Export: Significant supply disruption post-2022 sanctions on Russia
- CPLP relevance: Standard in Angolan, Mozambican, Guinea-Bissau forces carrying AK-74 variants; procurement now complex due to sanctions

### 7.62x51mm NATO (.308 Winchester)
- Velocity: 838 m/s | Energy: 3,438J | GPMG and DMR standard
- Producers: Lake City Army Ammunition Plant (USA), BAE (UK), Nammo (Norway/Finland), CBC (Brazil), IMI (Israel)
- ARIA note: Common currency in multi-market procurement — can supply both sniper and GPMG systems with single calibre logistics

### 7.62x54mmR (Russian rimmed)
- Oldest military calibre still in active service (since 1891)
- Used in: SVD Dragunov, PKM, vintage Mosin-Nagant
- Producers: Russia (primary), Bulgaria, Romania, China
- Supply risk: Russian origin; sanctions impact procurement
- CPLP relevance: Critical for CPLP African forces using Soviet-legacy weapons — understanding supply chain is essential for BD

### .50 BMG (12.7x99mm NATO)
- Energy: 18,000J | Effective range: 1,800m anti-personnel / 2,000m
- Producers: Lake City (USA), BAE Radway Green (UK), Nammo, FNB (Belgium)
- Cost: $3-8 USD per round depending on variant
- Export: ITAR/EAR controlled; US State Dept licence for M2 + ammunition

### 12.7x108mm (Russian)
- Russian/Soviet equivalent of .50 BMG
- Used in: DSHK, NSV, Kord HMGs; KPV 14.5mm for heavier applications
- ARIA note: Angola and Mozambique operate significant stocks of 12.7mm and 14.5mm crew-served weapons; replacement ammo procurement is ongoing opportunity

## Medium & Large Calibre Ammunition

### 20mm (Various)
- Used in: M61 Vulcan (aviation), M163 VADS (ground AA), Rh 202
- NATO standard: 20x102mm (Vulcan), 20x139mm (Rh 202)

### 23mm (Soviet)
- Used in: ZU-23-2 twin cannon (ubiquitous in Africa), ZSU-23-4
- ARIA note: ZU-23-2 is arguably the most common AA system in Sub-Saharan Africa; ammunition is a consistent procurement item

### 25mm (NATO)
- Used in: M242 Bushmaster (Bradley IFV), Bushmaster variants
- Penetration: Armour-piercing rounds penetrate 60mm RHA at 1,000m
- Cost: $30-60 per round (APDS variants)

### 30mm (Various)
- NATO: 30x173mm (Bushmaster II, Mauser BK-27 aviation)
- Soviet: 30x165mm (2A42 — BMP-2, BTR-80A; GSh-30 aviation)

### 40mm Grenade (NATO)
- M203/M320 launcher standard (40x46mm — low velocity)
- Mk19 automatic grenade launcher (40x53mm — high velocity)
- Producers: Nammo, Day & Zimmermann (USA), Chemring (UK)
- ARIA note: 40mm is a frequent procurement item for any force modernising from AK/RPK to M16/M4 pattern weapons

## Artillery Ammunition

### 105mm (NATO standard howitzer)
- M1 HE: High explosive, effective radius 50m | M67 HEAT: Anti-armour penetration
- Used in: M119A3 (USA), L118 (UK), G7 (South Africa)
- Range: 11.5km (unassisted) / 19km (RAP)
- CPLP relevance: South African G7 is available for Portuguese-speaking market procurement without US ITAR controls

### 155mm (NATO standard heavy artillery)
- The most important artillery calibre in current global conflict
- M795: Standard NATO HE shell | Weight: 43.2kg
- M982 Excalibur: GPS/INS guided, CEP <4m, range 40-57km
- BONUS: Swedish anti-armour sensor-fused munition (155mm)
- DPICM: Dual-Purpose Improved Conventional Munition (cluster) — banned by many nations; significant export restrictions
- Cost: Standard HE $800-1,200 | Excalibur $112,000+ per round
- Producers: BAE Systems (USA/UK/Sweden), Nammo, General Dynamics, Rheinmetall, Denel (South Africa)

### 122mm (Soviet/Russian)
- Used in: BM-21 Grad MLRS, D-30 howitzer, D-74 gun
- Still the most common artillery calibre in African conflicts
- Producers: Russia, China, Bulgaria, Romania, Egypt, North Korea
- Range (D-30): 15.4km | Range (BM-21): 20-40km depending on round
- CPLP relevance: Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau all operate Soviet-legacy 122mm systems; ammo supply critical

### 152mm (Soviet/Russian)
- Used in: 2S3 Akatsiya, 2A65 Msta-B howitzer
- Equivalent role to NATO 155mm in Soviet-bloc armies
- ARIA note: Increasingly difficult to procure post-2022; clients with 152mm systems face significant supply risk

## Anti-Tank & Rocket Ammunition

### RPG-7 (USSR — 1961)
- Launcher calibre: 40mm | Warhead calibre: 40-105mm (various)
- PG-7VL: 500mm RHA penetration | TBG-7V: Thermobaric
- Range: 200m (moving target) / 500m (stationary)
- Most widely used anti-armour weapon in history
- CPLP relevance: Standard in ALL CPLP African forces; ammunition procurement is a consistent and significant market opportunity

### PG-7V Warhead Variants
- PG-7V (original): 260mm penetration — defeats most APCs
- PG-7VL (improved): 500mm penetration — defeats older MBTs
- PG-7VR (tandem): 600mm behind ERA — defeats modern MBTs with ERA
- TBG-7V (thermobaric): Anti-personnel/bunker clearing role
- OG-7V (fragmentation): Anti-personnel in urban environments

### Javelin (USA — 1996)
- Man-portable fire-and-forget ATGM | Range: 2,500m (top attack) / 4,000m (direct attack, FGM-148F)
- Penetration: 600mm+ behind ERA (classified exact figure)
- Cost: ~$176,000 per missile; $240,000 per CLU
- Guidance: IIR (Imaging InfraRed) — operator need not track after fire
- Combat history: Iraq, Afghanistan, Ukraine (2022) — highly effective against T-72/80/90 tanks
- Export: ITAR controlled; FMS required; significant backlog post-Ukraine

### NLAW (UK/Sweden — 2009)
- Range: 20-800m | Single-shot disposable
- Guidance: PLOS (Predicted Line Of Sight) + overfly top attack
- Cost: ~$40,000 per unit
- ARIA note: More affordable alternative to Javelin; relevant for African client force capability assessment

### Kornet (Russia — 1998) / Fagot / Konkurs
- Kornet: 5,000-10,000m range; tandem warhead; laser beam riding
- Penetration: 1,000-1,300mm behind ERA
- Still widely exported by Russia; common in Middle East and Africa

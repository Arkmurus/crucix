# ARIA Military Hardware — Armoured Fighting Vehicles
Source: Arkmurus Hardware Library v1.0 | Classification: ARKMURUS INTERNAL

## ARIA AFV Doctrine

Tank and AFV procurement is the highest-value single item in any military procurement programme. ARIA assesses:
1. Protection level (armour basis weight, ERA, active protection)
2. Firepower (main gun calibre, ammunition types, fire control)
3. Mobility (power-to-weight ratio, terrain performance, reliability)
4. Logistics (fuel type, spare parts, crew training requirements)
5. Total lifecycle cost (acquisition + 20-year operating cost)
6. Combat record (verified kills vs losses in real conflicts)
7. Export control status and supplier political reliability

## Main Battle Tanks — Comprehensive Comparison

### T-72 (USSR — 1971, multiple variants to present)
- Main gun: 125mm 2A46M smoothbore | Autoloader: Yes (carousel) — crew of 3
- Protection: Steel + composite (T-72B3); Kontakt-5 ERA available
- Mobility: 780hp diesel | 60km/h road | 500km range | Weight: 44-46 tonnes
- Cost (T-72B3): ~$1.5-2M per unit
- Known weaknesses: Autoloader carousel explosion ("jack-in-the-box" effect when ammunition penetrated); fire control inferior to NATO
- CPLP relevance: Angola and Mozambique operate T-54/55/72 variants; upgrade and sustainment is ongoing procurement opportunity
- Oryx verified losses (Ukraine 2022-2024): 3,000+ T-72 variants lost (highest verified loss of any tank type in modern warfare)

### T-80 (USSR — 1976)
- Main gun: 125mm 2A46 | Turbine engine (gas) | 1,000hp; 70km/h
- Protection: Improved glacis; Kontakt-5/Relikt ERA
- Weakness: High fuel consumption (turbine); poor cold-weather start reliability
- Cost: ~$3M per unit

### T-90 / T-90M Proryv (Russia — 1992/2017)
- Main gun: 125mm 2A46M-5 | Autoloader
- T-90M upgrades: Improved Relikt ERA, Nakidka anti-radar coating, improved sights (Sosna-U thermal sight), new turret armour array
- Cost: ~$4.5M (T-90S export)
- Export nations: India (1,000+), Algeria, Azerbaijan, Vietnam, Uganda, Turkmenistan

### Leopard 2 (Germany — 1979, variants to 2A7+)
- Main gun: 120mm Rheinmetall L/55 (2A6/2A7) | Crew: 4 (no autoloader — separated ammunition improves survivability)
- Protection: 2A7+: Add-on composite package front; Trophy APS optional
- Mobility: MTU 873 diesel 1,500hp; 68km/h; excellent cross-country | Weight: 62-68 tonnes
- Cost: ~$10-13M per unit (2A6/2A7)
- Fire control: Significantly superior to Russian equivalents; integrated battle management
- ARIA note: NATO standard export tank; German export licence required; EU Common Position and SITCL apply to brokering

### Challenger 2 (UK — 1994)
- Main gun: L30A1 120mm rifled (unique — fires HESH, cannot fire NATO-standard smoothbore ammunition)
- Protection: Chobham/Dorchester 2 — considered among best passive armour in the world
- Crew: 4 | Weight: 75 tonnes | Limitation: Rifled gun limits ammunition compatibility with NATO; logistical island
- Cost: ~$8M per unit
- Combat history: Gulf War (1991 — no combat losses to enemy fire); Ukraine (2023 — first confirmed losses)

### M1A2 Abrams (USA — 1980, SEPv3 current)
- Main gun: 120mm M256 smoothbore | Crew: 4
- Protection: Chobham + DU (depleted uranium) insert — DU inserts classified; export versions without DU
- Mobility: AGT-1500 gas turbine 1,500hp; 67km/h; 426km range (limited) | Weight: 66-73 tonnes
- Cost: ~$10-15M (M1A2 SEPv3)
- Weakness: Turbine fuel consumption very high; maintenance intensive
- Export: ITAR controlled; FMS only; political conditions apply

### K2 Black Panther (South Korea — 2014)
- Main gun: 120mm Rheinmetall L/55 compatible | Crew: 3 (autoloader)
- Mobility: 1,500hp; 70km/h; excellent cross-country (hydropneumatic) | Weight: 55 tonnes
- Cost: ~$8.5M
- Export: Poland contracted 1,000 K2/K2PL variants — largest European tank deal in decades
- ARIA note: Rising global export profile; competitive alternative to European platforms

## Infantry Fighting Vehicles & APCs

### BMP-2 (USSR — 1980)
- Role: IFV | Crew: 3 + 7 infantry | Main armament: 30mm 2A42 autocannon + AT-5 Spandrel ATGM
- Protection: RHA steel — defeats 23mm AP frontally
- Cost: ~$500K (used); up to $2M (upgraded)
- CPLP relevance: Angola, Mozambique operate BMP-1/2 variants; upgrade programmes are live procurement opportunities

### Bradley M2/M3 (USA — 1981)
- Role: IFV | Crew: 3 + 6 infantry | Main armament: 25mm M242 Bushmaster + TOW ATGM
- Protection: Aluminium hull + applique steel; significantly improved with BUSK III armour package
- Cost: ~$3.5M | New M2A4: ~$7M | Export: ITAR controlled

### CV90 (Sweden — 1993)
- Role: IFV | Multiple variants (35mm/40mm Bofors armament)
- Protection: Among best-protected IFVs in service | Weight: 35 tonnes | Cost: ~$5-7M

### BTR-80/82A (Russia)
- Role: APC/IFV | Wheeled 8x8 | BTR-82A armament: 30mm 2A72 autocannon
- Wide use across CPLP African states; very common in Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau
- Cost: ~$1-1.5M | Very good mobility on African terrain

### MRAP Family (USA/Various)
- Cougar, MaxxPro, MRAP-ATV — developed for IED resistance
- V-hull design: deflects blast downward rather than up through crew
- Relevant for markets where IED/mine threat is primary (Mozambique Cabo Delgado, Sahel region)

## Wheeled Armoured Vehicles — African Market Relevance

### RG-31 Nyala (South Africa)
- Manufacturer: BAE Systems South Africa | Role: MRAP | Crew: 2 + 8 | Weight: 7.6 tonnes
- Protection: Blast-resistant V-hull; STANAG 1 ballistic protection | Range: 1,000km
- ARIA note: South African manufacture — significant for CPLP markets as South Africa is a key supplier without ITAR constraints

### Casspir (South Africa — 1978)
- The original mine-protected vehicle; inspired all subsequent MRAP design
- South African COIN experience in Angola directly contributed to design

### Ratel IFV (South Africa)
- 6x6 wheeled IFV | 20mm autocannon | Proven in Angola (SADF, 1970s-1980s)
- ARIA note: Understanding Ratel operational history is essential for any Angola-adjacent BD — it fought there extensively

### Oshkosh JLTV (USA)
- Joint Light Tactical Vehicle — M1114 Humvee replacement
- Protected mobility; modular; lighter than MRAP | Export: ITAR controlled
